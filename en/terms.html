TERMS OF USE · 28 OCTOBER 2026

Clear terms for using GUARDIANA on the web.

GUARDIANA is a set of services used from this website, with no account and nothing to install: NÚCLEO, the ZERO browser (demonstration version), Orbit, Communications, the X-rays and the Fines. Today they are free: nothing is charged.

1. What it is

NÚCLEO is the entry point: it guides you to what you need. The ZERO browser opens each website in a remote bubble and shows you who it connects to, so that you can decide what to cut. Orbit is a map of the internet’s infrastructure: cables, data centres and satellites. Communications is for sending messages, voice notes and files, and for making calls, end-to-end encrypted between two browsers. The X-rays and the Fines are reports and cases, each with its source and date.

Today nothing is charged: there are no paid plans, no subscriptions and no payment details to give. The program for Windows includes a 7-day trial with every feature; after that it keeps working free at the GUARDIANA Free level, and the full features will need the subscription, which cannot be purchased yet. If there is ever a paid service, these terms will say so beforehand, with its price and its date, and nobody will start paying without choosing to.

2. What it does not promise

  • GUARDIANA shows you connections and cuts what it recognises, but no tool detects all the tracking, fraud or dangerous content on the internet. We do not guarantee total security, nor protection against anything in particular.
  • It is not an antivirus, parental control or a VPN, and it does not provide anonymity: GUARDIANA’s servers see your IP address when you use them (as the Privacy policy explains).
  • ZERO is a demonstration version: it may be slow, fail or be unavailable, and some websites may not work inside the bubble.
  • The services may change or be interrupted, for maintenance or for causes outside our control, without prior notice.
  • What Orbit, the X-rays and the Fines show is information with its source and date, not legal, technical or financial advice.

3. Permitted use

Use GUARDIANA for lawful purposes. Do not use it to attack, overload or test third-party systems without permission, to send illegal content or to impersonate another person. ZERO always blocks known dangerous websites and internal addresses, and GUARDIANA may cut off access to ZERO or to Communications for anyone who abuses them, for example through massive automated use.

4. Your part

What you do and what you send with GUARDIANA is your responsibility. In Communications, compare the safety number with the other person through another channel before sending anything sensitive: that is what proves that nobody has got in the middle. Encryption does not protect a device or a browser that is already compromised, nor does it prevent the recipient from keeping copies.

5. Open source

The code of this website is published under the GNU AGPL-3.0 licence at github.com/guardianagroup/guardianagroup.com: anyone can read it and check that it does what we say. The code licence does not grant the right to use the GUARDIANA name or its logos.

The PDF report of “The journey of your data” is also put together on your device, with html2canvas (Niklas von Hertzen, MIT licence). Orbit’s typefaces (Instrument Serif, Schibsted Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk and IBM Plex Mono) are served from this same site under their licence, the SIL Open Font License 1.1. “With / Without GUARDIANA” shows a measurement of our own: on 23 October 2026, the home pages of eleven sports and news dailies were opened from Colombia, in a browser with no blockers and accepting cookies whenever the website asked for them, and every domain of another company that the browser connected to was recorded; the company and the country of its headquarters are published only when at least two independent reviews agree, and the paths, along the map’s cables and satellites, are illustrative: they reach the country of that headquarters and do not indicate the route each piece of data took. “With GUARDIANA” applies GUARDIANA’s rules to that same measurement (advertising, tracking and measurement are cut); it is not a measurement of your device.

ZERO opens each website in a remote GUARDIANA bubble (a Google Chrome browser — or Chromium, BSD licence, if Chrome is not available — driven with Playwright, Apache 2.0 licence). It always stops dangerous websites and shows each website’s advertising, tracking and cookie notices; with Google or Brave it cuts them when the person chooses to, and with Tor it cuts them from the start, together with the advertising, measurement and cookie-notice companies identified in GUARDIANA’s database. It uses public lists that are rebuilt every week from their repositories: EasyList and EasyPrivacy (GPL-3.0 or CC BY-SA 3.0, easylist.to) and the uBlock Origin lists (uAssets: privacy, badware, filters, unbreak, privacy-removeparam, annoyances-cookies and lan-block; GPL-3.0). The company behind each domain and the country of its headquarters come from Wikidata (CC0) and from GUARDIANA’s own database, which publishes only what at least two independent reviews confirm; when it is not known, the screen says “Other company”. What ZERO reports about an ad auction is what the page itself declares (Prebid, the consent notice and the ad server): it always says what is declared and what cannot be known. With Tor, ZERO goes out through the Tor network (The Tor Project, BSD licence) and opens only the regular web: .onion addresses are blocked on the server, except for verified official services (today, Brave’s, with the address that Brave published on its official forum: community.brave.app, “Update: New .onion addresses for Brave website and Brave Search”). With Tor, search is Brave Search through Tor. No website opened in ZERO can connect to the bubble’s server, to private networks or to the provider’s internal data. No filter detects all illegal or dangerous content on the internet. The PDF report is generated in the person’s own browser, with no external libraries, and is not sent to anyone. The record of each session summarises what ZERO did and is sealed by GUARDIANA’s own server with a temporary key that is not kept: it is for your own reference, not evidence before third parties or legal advice.

Communications encrypts in your own browser, with the cryptography built into the browser (Web Crypto) and, for ML-KEM-768, the open library @noble/post-quantum 0.7.1 (MIT licence, by Paul Miller), served from this same site: an ECDSA P-256 and ECDH P-256 identity created on your device, a hybrid session agreement with three ECDH P-256 exchanges and one ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203), the invitation secret and HKDF-SHA256, and a different AES-256-GCM key for each message and for each file chunk. The invitation is a random 9-digit code valid for 10 minutes and for a single use: the conversation secret is deposited on GUARDIANA’s server, locked with a key derived from the code (PBKDF2-SHA256, 300,000 iterations); the server does not keep it unlocked and limits lookup attempts, and its protection depends on the code, which is why the code expires after 10 minutes and works only once. The server relays encrypted bytes between the two people, keeps no names or keys and only holds, end-to-end encrypted, the messages for someone who is not connected, until they are delivered or for 7 days at most; it does see IP addresses, the room, the times and the traffic volume, like any server: this is not anonymity. Voice notes are recorded in your browser and travel like any other file. What the screen calls the “safety number” is the fingerprint of the two identities: comparing it through another channel is what proves that nobody got in the middle; until then, trust rests on the code. “End” deletes the conversation in your browser and requests its deletion in the other person’s: it does not reach anything already saved to a disk. Received files remain in the browser without encryption of their own. GUARDIANA does not open or analyse the content (it is encrypted), so it does not detect viruses: it checks that each message and each chunk arrives intact and untampered with, and stops the transfer if not. No encryption protects a device, browser or app that is already compromised. In the normal session, the key agreement is post-quantum hybrid (ECDH P-256 + ML-KEM-768), but the identity signatures are still ECDSA P-256, which is not post-quantum; if the other device uses an earlier version, that session is ECDH only and the security log warns about it. The shielded session, coming soon, will use the algorithms of the CNSA 2.0 suite with the same library: a new identity in each session with ECDSA P-384, ECDH P-384 and ML-DSA-87 (FIPS 204); a hybrid agreement with three ECDH P-384 exchanges, one ML-KEM-1024 (FIPS 203), the invitation secret and HKDF-SHA-384; and both signatures, the classical one and the post-quantum one, over the whole negotiation and over the integrity of what is sent. Its invitation is a link or a QR code with a 256-bit secret that is not deposited on the server; the safety number can be compared at any time (“Security and verification”); the keys are renewed every 5 minutes and, when the session ends, they are deleted from the page’s memory, although the browser and the system may keep copies that we cannot guarantee will disappear. Voice calls and video calls always go through a GUARDIANA call server (TURN), the one closest to each person (Nuremberg or Miami), which sees the IP addresses, the times and the volume, not the audio or the picture: the call negotiation travels inside the end-to-end encrypted channel, and the audio and picture travel with DTLS-SRTP, the standard encryption for browser calls; in Chrome and Edge a post-quantum layer is added when both devices have it enabled, and the call screen indicates it. Voice changing will come with the shielded session: it will be applied in your browser before sending and will change the pitch, not the accent, the words or the way of speaking.

A1. What it was

GUARDIANA is an open-source program (GPL‑3.0‑or‑later licence) for Windows and macOS that turns your PC into the DNS guardian of your home. There is one plan, and it covers both programs, GUARDIANA and GUARDIANA ZERO: each one works in full for a 7-day trial, with no card and no account, and after that you need a subscription, monthly or yearly, which you can cancel whenever you like.

What happens if you do not subscribe when the trial ends: GUARDIANA does not switch off. It moves to GUARDIANA Free, for good, and keeps watching this computer with the DNS as it is: the live globe, every program’s DNS with its country, company and category, your blocks and their undo, and the last 24 hours of history. The rest is Plus: the whole history, up to 5 computers, alerts, automatic blocks by category and lists, certificate and rights letters, reports, Home Mode, encrypted communications and GUARDIANA ZERO. If you had Home Mode switched on, the other devices in the house keep getting answers so that nobody is left without internet, but Free does not look at them or record them. What it had already recorded stays whole on your disk and you can export it as CSV, JSON or PDF whenever you like; nothing is deleted, not even years later, and none of it ever reaches us.

It includes GUARDIANA ZERO. The subscription also includes GUARDIANA ZERO, the browser for Windows 10 and 11, which has its own 7-day trial, with no card and separate from GUARDIANA’s: the same key works for both programs. If its trial ends without a subscription, the browser keeps opening pages, but without its protection (it does not cut trackers, advertising or data brokers, does not clean addresses, does not watch your marked data and does not carry out mandates or redactions).

The code is open and will remain so. It is published in full under GPL‑3.0: if you know how to compile it, the program is yours, free, with no time limit and without asking our permission. What is paid for here is the ready-made program — compiled, signed, tested on Windows and macOS, with the lists renewed in each version — and having a person who answers.

GUARDIANA sees service names. It does not see the content, or which app asked for them on a phone. It acts only on your home Wi‑Fi. It is not an antivirus or parental control. And it does not see what bypasses DNS, such as a VPN or an encrypted DNS of its own. We do not guarantee “total security”, nor protection against anything in particular. The full list of limits is on the website and in the program itself. Using it is your decision and your responsibility, under the terms of the GPL‑3.0 licence.
