GUIDE · THE PANEL

What each category and each colour means

Every row of the panel carries a category label with a coloured dot. This page says, word by word, what each one means and where it comes from. Everything here is an observation, never a verdict: GUARDIANA sees names, not content, and calls nothing “malicious”.

The three colours

Green: normal

What a service needs to work: its website, its server, your account, updates, the content it downloads, your home network. No need to look at it.

Orange: worth a look

It measures what you do, serves ads or nobody knows it: advertising, telemetry, measurement, ads, unclassified. Not bad in itself; it is what you should look at, because you know what you opened.

Red: alert

A public list recognises it as tracking, the company is registered as a data broker, or the device tries to dodge the watch (DNS evasion). It is where to start if you are going to block something.

The colour comes from what the label already says, not from an opinion of the program: the list that recognises it, the word in the name itself, the broker registry, the evasion signal. Two rows with the same label have the same colour, unless one belongs to a company registered as a data broker or carries the DNS evasion signal: that one goes red, whatever the label says.

The signals

Besides the category, each row may carry one sentence per signal. Signals look at behaviour (when, how much, how regularly), not at the name.

  • Beacon. It contacts the same destination at regular intervals, like a heartbeat: every 5 minutes, every hour. That is how programs that report on themselves talk… and also the ones that check the mail.
  • New destination. This device had never looked for this service. Normal the day you install something; odd at three in the morning.
  • Out of hours. It talks at hours this device does not usually talk at. GUARDIANA learns each device's hours for three days before saying so.
  • DNS evasion. It tries to resolve names on its own, out of GUARDIANA's sight (its own encrypted DNS). GUARDIANA does not see where it goes; it says so, and the row goes red.
  • Volume. It is asking much more than usual: several times the median of its active hours.

Block and unblock

Every row has its button. Block makes that name “not exist” for that device: the question does not leave home. Unblock undoes that block, even when no rule for that name caused it: if Guard Mode did, the name goes into the scope you declared for that device; if a broad rule (home-wide, or a category) did, a rule that allows that name on that device is created, and it wins because it is more specific. Nothing is blocked without your decision, and everything is undone in one click under “Rules”. A block does not erase what was already sent, and does not stop whoever uses their own encrypted DNS or a VPN.

What this is not

It is not an antivirus, not parental control, and it sees no content: it sees which names each device tries to talk to, and tells you in the words above. What GUARDIANA does not do is written, dated, in the open code.
