AI4

€15m · annulledOpenAI · 2026

ChatGPT · the Italian fine happened, and a court struck it down

Italy's Garante fined OpenAI in December 2024 for processing data without a clear legal basis, not reporting the March 2023 leak and not checking the age of whoever signs in. On 18 March 2026 the Court of Rome annulled it. It is worth reading why: the court did not say ChatGPT was fine, it said the case belonged to the Irish authority and not the Italian one, because OpenAI had placed its European seat there. It fell on who was judging, not on what was being judged.

€158,000Character.AI · 2026

Virtual characters, minors inside, and no age check

Italy's Garante fined Character Technologies, the company behind Character.AI, a service where anyone — a child included — creates virtual characters and chats with them. It found insufficient information for users, an impact assessment done late and an EU representative appointed late, on top of failings in protecting minors and in age verification. The fine is small; the orders are not: it has to make age verification work, stop a blocked minor from signing up again, and set minors' profiles to private by default.

€5mReplika · 2025

A companion AI, with no legal basis and no age check

Replika is a chatbot that plays the friend or the partner. Italy's Garante fined its company, Luka Inc., for processing data without a valid legal basis, not explaining what it did with it, and having no way to check age: a name, an email and a gender were enough to get in. The authority pointed to the risk to minors.

GARANTE (ITALY) · 19 MAY 2025

€30.5mClearview AI · 2024

Thirty billion faces scraped off the internet

The Dutch authority fined Clearview AI for building a database of more than 30 billion photos of faces scraped from the internet and turned into one biometric code per person, without anyone's permission and without telling anyone. On top of the fine, up to five million more in penalty payments. And an unusual warning: the authority said using Clearview is illegal and that companies doing so can expect fines of their own.

Court-approved settlement in Lopez v. Apple: the plaintiffs alleged that Siri recorded conversations without intentional activation and that human contractors reviewed them. Apple paid without admitting wrongdoing.

€98.6mApple · 2025

The same privacy prompt, fined in Italy too

Italy's competition authority fined Apple for abuse of a dominant position over the same prompt: whoever builds an app has to ask for permission twice for the same thing, once in Apple's window and once in their own. Apple, in its own apps, does not go through that. The investigation was run in coordination with the European Commission and the Italian data protection authority.

€150mApple · 2025

Fined over its own privacy button, not for spying

This one has to be read carefully, because it is the opposite of what it looks like: Apple was not fined for tracking, but for how it imposed its “Ask App Not to Track” window on everyone else. The French competition authority said the goal is not the problem; the problem is that refusing takes one click and accepting takes two, that the rule was not applied evenly, and that Apple did not apply it to itself. A fine for abuse of a dominant position between April 2021 and July 2023.

Google1

€325mGoogle · 2025

Cookies when you create the account, and ads between your emails

Two things in one penalty. When creating a Google account it was harder to refuse advertising cookies than to accept them, and it was not made clear that accepting was a condition of using the service: 74 million accounts. And anyone with Gmail's “smart features” turned on had advertising messages shaped like emails slipped in between their private ones, without being asked: 53 million people.

CNIL (FRANCE) · 1 SEP 2025

€251mMeta · 2024

The 2018 session keys: 29 million accounts left wide open

A fine for the breach Meta reported in September 2018: a flaw exposed the session keys of about 29 million accounts worldwide, along with name, email, phone, sex, workplace and each account's posts and groups. The Irish authority closed two inquiries with 251 million in total.

€1.2bnMeta · 2023

The largest data fine ever handed down

One point two billion euros against Meta, the owner of Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, for continuing to send European users' data to the United States without protection equivalent to Europe's. It is the largest penalty in the history of the European data protection regulation.

LinkedIn · targeted ads without a permission that held up

Ireland's data protection authority fined LinkedIn, which belongs to Microsoft, for analysing the behaviour of people with a profile and using it for targeted advertising without a valid legal basis. Besides the fine, it ordered LinkedIn to bring the processing into compliance.

Antivirus1

$16.5mAntivirus · 2024

Avast · sold the browsing of the people it protected

The FTC found that Avast, while promising to block tracking, sold detailed, re-identifiable browsing data to more than 100 third parties through its subsidiary Jumpshot between 2014 and 2020. It is banned from selling browsing data for advertising. Avast disagrees with the characterisation of the facts.

Shops1

€150mSHEIN · 2025

Trackers before you touch anything, and a “refuse” that did not refuse

The CNIL fined SHEIN's Irish subsidiary because the site dropped trackers, several of them for advertising, the moment someone arrived: before they had touched the cookie banner at all. And when they pressed “Refuse all” or withdrew consent, new trackers were dropped anyway and the ones already there kept on being read. Around 12 million visits a month from France.

CNIL (FRANCE) · 1 SEP 2025

Data for sale1

BanData for sale · 2026

Kochava · sold the precise location of hundreds of millions of phones

The FTC bans Kochava and its subsidiary Collective Data Solutions from selling, licensing or sharing sensitive location data without affirmative express consent. According to the complaint, the precise location they sold could be used to trace a person's movements, including visits to health facilities and places of worship. No fine: a ban, plus the duty to tell anyone who asks which businesses their location was sold to.

FTC (USA) · 4 MAY 2026

Your phone company1

€42mFree · 2026

Free and Free Mobile · 24 million contracts exposed, IBANs included

27 million against Free Mobile and 15 against Free. An attacker got in during October 2024 and reached data from 24 million subscriber contracts; for people who were customers of both companies, the IBAN as well. The CNIL pointed to weak authentication on remote access, insufficient detection, data kept from contracts long since terminated, and a notice to those affected that did not explain the consequences. More than 2,500 complaints.
