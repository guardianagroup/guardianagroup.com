A fine for the breach Meta reported in September 2018: a flaw exposed the session keys of about 29 million accounts worldwide, along with name, email, phone, sex, workplace and each account's posts and groups. The Irish authority closed two inquiries with 251 million in total.

Facial recognition on photos without consent

Texas sued Meta in 2022 for applying facial recognition to photos uploaded to Facebook (the 'Tag Suggestions' feature, on by default) without Texans' consent, in violation of the state's 2009 biometric law. Meta will pay $500 million in the first year and $225 million a year from 2025 to 2028. Meta denies any wrongdoing.

The largest data fine ever handed down

One point two billion euros against Meta, the owner of Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, for continuing to send European users' data to the United States without protection equivalent to Europe's. It is the largest penalty in the history of the European data protection regulation.

Personalised ads without a valid legal basis

The DPC and the EDPB concluded that Meta could not rely on 'contract' as the legal basis for behavioural advertising on Facebook and Instagram. It also breached transparency rules by not clearly explaining which legal basis it was using. It was given three months to bring its processing into compliance.

In addition to the fine, it issued a reprimand and ordered the processing to be brought into compliance.

Design failures that let user data be scraped

In its inquiry into Facebook 'data scraping', the DPC found breaches of GDPR Articles 25(1) and 25(2) (data protection by design and by default). It imposed fines totalling €265 million, a reprimand and an order to bring processing into compliance.

Friends' data shared with apps despite privacy settings

According to the FTC, Facebook violated its 2012 order by deceiving users about their control over their privacy: it shared friends' data with app developers despite their settings and used phone numbers provided for two-factor verification for advertising. Location data kept too long and used for ads

The DPC found that between May 2018 and February 2020 Google kept location data longer than necessary and used it to target ads: processing was unlawful in 'Location Accuracy' and retention was improper in 'Web & App Activity' and 'Location History'. Cookies when you create the account, and ads between your emails

Two things in one penalty. When creating a Google account it was harder to refuse advertising cookies than to accept them, and it was not made clear that accepting was a condition of using the service: 74 million accounts. And anyone with Gmail's “smart features” turned on had advertising messages shaped like emails slipped in between their private ones, without being asked: 53 million people.

€150MAutorité DE LA Concurrence (France)2025 · Apple

Fined over its own privacy button, not for spying

This one has to be read carefully, because it is the opposite of what it looks like: Apple was not fined for tracking, but for how it imposed its “Ask App Not to Track” window on everyone else. The French competition authority said the goal is not the problem; the problem is that refusing takes one click and accepting takes two, that the rule was not applied evenly, and that Apple did not apply it to itself. A fine for abuse of a dominant position between April 2021 and July 2023.
