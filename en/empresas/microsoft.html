COMPANY PROFILE

Microsoft

Headquarters: Redmond, United States · Listed: NASDAQ: MSFT

7 fines or sanctions1 data breach

What it does and how it makes money

Microsoft sells software and cloud services to people, businesses and governments, such as Windows, Microsoft 365, Azure, Xbox and the Bing search engine; it also owns the professional network LinkedIn.

In fiscal year 2026 its revenue was split between Productivity and Business Processes (USD 139,996 million), Intelligent Cloud (USD 137,791 million) and More Personal Computing (USD 54,052 million). [1]

What data it collects about you

According to regulators, Bing placed advertising cookies without consent, Xbox collected personal data from children at sign-up, and LinkedIn used members' data for behavioural advertising.

How it turns your data into money

Microsoft collects data from your interactions with its products and uses cookies, web beacons and pixels, including from partners and third parties, for personalised advertising. It states it may use your data to 'advertise and market to you', with promotional communications and offers, and that ads may be personalised using information about you over time; it says advertising helps support some of its products. If you search Bing for 'running shoes', you may see related ads in other Microsoft products; you can opt out of personalised ads on its preferences page. [2]

Where it is incorporated

Microsoft Corporation, a corporation incorporated in the State of Washington (USA) with principal offices at One Microsoft Way, Redmond, Washington, listed on Nasdaq; founded in 1975. [3]

Taxes: where it pays them

Its 2026 annual report states that its tax rate is below the U.S. federal rate primarily because it produces and distributes products through its 'foreign regional operating center in Ireland', taxed at a rate lower than the U.S. rate, which generated 81% of its foreign income before tax in 2026 and 2025 (83% in 2024). In addition, on September 26, 2023 the IRS issued Notices of Proposed Adjustment seeking $28.9 billion plus penalties and interest for 2004-2013, primarily over intercompany transfer pricing; Microsoft disagrees and is contesting them. [3]

Competition and monopoly

In March 2004 the European Union fined Microsoft €497 million for abusing its dominance with Windows. On September 12, 2025 the European Commission accepted Microsoft's commitments as legally binding for up to 10 years to close the case over tying Teams to Office 365/Microsoft 365 (Slack complaint): offering the suites without Teams at a discount, letting customers switch to Teams-free packages and easing interoperability and data portability. [4]

Fines and sanctions

Each is what an authority decided on its date; it does not prove what the company does today.

In total: 2,568.5 million EUR + 20 million USD

  • DPC (Irlanda) · Ireland · October 24, 2024310 million EUR

  • FTC · United States · June 5, 202320 million USD
    Settlement with the FTC: Xbox collected personal data from children without notifying or getting consent from parents and kept it for years (COPPA). These are the FTC's allegations, resolved by settlement. [6]
  • CNIL · France · December 202260 million EUR
    Placing advertising cookies on bing.com without prior consent and without a way to refuse them as easily as accepting them (Microsoft Ireland Operations). [7]
  • Comisión Europea · European Union · March 6, 2013561 million EUR
    Antitrust: breaking its commitment to show a browser choice screen; about 15.3 million Windows 7 SP1 users did not see it. [8]
