Six US newspapers: how much tracking each front page loads

CNN, The New York Times, Fox News, The Washington Post, USA Today and The Wall Street Journal, opened one by one in a freshly installed Chrome, 20 seconds each, with GUARDIANA recording every name the front page made the machine ask for.

How to read this. GUARDIANA writes down every name the machine asks to resolve (DNS). It sees names, not content. Each front page was opened alone, in a clean browser with no account, and measured from the inside.

In this test the one loading the least tracking was The New York Times (74 names against 214 for the one loading most). It is a result of these front pages on this day, not a general verdict.

What it says

A big US newspaper front page loads, in 20 seconds and without touching anything, between 74 and 214 distinct advertising and tracking names: ad networks (Google, Amazon, Taboola, Outbrain, Criteo, Rubicon, OpenX, PubMatic and more), audience measurement and identity synchronisers. USA Today and The Washington Post load the most: each made the machine ask for more than 300 distinct service names. The New York Times loads the fewest, with 74. CNN stands apart because from Colombia it serves its international edition.

What only a guardian on the machine sees

The browser shows the page; GUARDIANA writes down every name the page makes the machine ask for, including the ones no content blocker ever shows you:

  • Outside the browser: in each window the machine also looked up between 14 and 48 services belonging to other apps. They are not published (they are the test machine’s), but in your own panel you would see them one by one, next to the phones and the TV of the house.
  • Attempts to bypass the machine’s DNS (the «DNS evasion» signal): none in this test.
  • And what escapes it, it says so: traffic over its own encrypted DNS, a VPN or a private relay does not go through the machine’s DNS. GUARDIANA flags it when it detects it and writes it into its limits; it does not promise to see a hundred per cent.

What can be claimed

  • Opening the front page of a big US newspaper in Chrome with no blocker makes the machine talk to between 74 and 214 distinct advertising and tracking services, without the person doing anything.
  • All six load advertising and tracking before any consent: none showed a notice first, from Colombia.
  • The names repeat from one front page to the next: four appeared on all six (Google advertising, DoubleClick and AppNexus) and 66 on at least four of them. Amazon's ad network (amazon-adsystem.com) appeared on 5 of the six.

What cannot be claimed

  • That the machine went on to connect to those services, or that it sent anything: what is seen here is that the machine asked for the name. A connection almost always follows, but a browser also looks names up ahead of time and sometimes never uses them.
  • What is sent when there is a connection, or what anyone read: GUARDIANA sees names, not content.
  • That a site is "spying": these are documented advertising, measurement and delivery services, and each name is published.
  • That the one loading least is the best, nor that a low figure means no tracking: they are names, not intentions.

What you can switch off

  • In any browser: a content blocker (uBlock Origin or similar) stops most of these requests before they leave.
  • In Brave, Firefox, Safari and DuckDuckGo, the default tracking protection trims a part of this; the five-browser x-ray says how much.
  • With GUARDIANA, any of these names is cut from the panel the moment you point at it, with undo beside it; extending it to every device in the house, including the ones with no blocker (the TV, the phone), waits until Guardiana has been watching for a day.

Data and method

Full data: seis-diarios-de-eeuu-2026-09-17.csv. Method: each site opened alone with site/src/radiografia/navegar.mjs (Chrome, empty profile, no scrolling, 20 s + 15 s); names taken from the machine's GUARDIANA ledger for each time window, this machine's queries only; only the ones the page asked for are counted. The tools are in the program's repository (site/src/radiografia/); anyone can repeat the test with Guardiana installed.

Brands named as the names of their owners. If you are the owner and something here is inaccurate, write to hola@guardianagroup.com: it is corrected, with the date.
