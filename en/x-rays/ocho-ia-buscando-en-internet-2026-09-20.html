X-RAY · 20 SEPTEMBER 2026

The same eight AIs, asked something they cannot know: the tracking was already there

In the morning they were asked the capital of Colombia, which they all know by heart. An hour and a half later, the weather in Barranquilla right now, which none of them knows: every one has to go and look it up. The surprise is that almost nothing changes. Gemini loads the same six advertising services both times; Copilot the same four. What they load, they load on opening, not on searching. The one real change: ChatGPT goes from one to three, and the two new ones belong to Google.

How to read this. GUARDIANA writes down every name the machine asks to resolve (DNS). It sees names, not content: neither the question nor the answer. Each AI was opened alone in a fresh tab of the everyday Chrome, already signed in, the same question was typed into it, and the tab was closed. The machine’s name cache was emptied before each one. From what the machine asked for in that minute, Windows’ own names, the signed-in browser’s own traffic and the machine’s search engines are subtracted; what is left is what the page made it ask for. “Third party” here means the name is not that AI: it may belong to the same company or group, or to another company, and the page shows the two apart.

The record

  • Date: 20 September 2026 (Bogotá time), 11:23 to 11:33. About a 65-second window per AI.
  • Question typed into all eight: «Que tiempo hace ahora mismo en Barranquilla y que temperatura hay». Without accents or question marks, because the script types key by key.
  • Machine: a Windows 11 test PC, the everyday Chrome with the real profile and the sessions already signed in, GUARDIANA 0.2.3 as its DNS.
  • Nine AIs were measured; eight are published. Mistral is out again: its window ended on «Iniciar sesión», the sign-in screen.
  • Four of the eight changed their window title to the answer — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity — which is the proof that they answered. The other four never change title.
  • An earlier attempt, at 10:39, was thrown away whole: with the window stretched to 60 seconds the run took thirteen minutes, the machine locked the session at ten, and the keystrokes stopped arriving. Eight of the nine measured zero, which is what a run that types nowhere measures. Since then the script has a guard that aborts if it cannot bring Chrome to the front.
  • 8,000 events in the ledger export these counts come from.

What it says

The idea was that forcing them to search would open a new world: the search engine behind them, the pages they fetch, the measurement of each query. It did not happen. Gemini loads its six advertising services with the easy question and with the hard one, the same six name by name; Copilot, the same four. What they load does not depend on what you ask: it is there from the moment the page opens. The exception is ChatGPT, which goes from one third party to three, and the two new ones are t1.gstatic.com and t3.gstatic.com, the thumbnails of the search results: you ask ChatGPT, and your machine ends up talking to Google. Claude and Meta AI repeat zero. And Grok repeats the other end: fourteen.

Why this x-ray matters even though it finds nothing new

A measurement that repeats is a measurement. Gemini with six and six, and Copilot with four and one, come out the same with two different questions at two times of day: that says what is counted is real and not the noise of the minute.

And the result nobody expected is a result too: an AI's tracking does not switch on when you ask it something hard. It was already on when you opened the page.

What only a guardian on the machine sees

The browser shows the page; GUARDIANA writes down every name the page makes the machine ask for, including the ones no content blocker ever shows you:

  • Outside the browser: in each window the machine also looked up between 1 and 3 services belonging to other apps. They are not published (they are the test machine’s), but in your own panel you would see them one by one, next to the phones and the TV of the house.
  • Attempts to bypass the machine’s DNS (the “DNS evasion” signal): none in this test.
  • And it says what escapes it: traffic over its own encrypted DNS, a VPN or a private relay does not go through the machine’s DNS. GUARDIANA flags it when it detects it and writes it into its limits; it does not promise to see a hundred per cent.

What can be claimed

  • With a question that forces a search, Gemini made the machine ask for the same six advertising and tracking names as with one that does not.
  • ChatGPT made it ask for two Google names that did not appear with the easy question: the thumbnails of the search results.
  • Claude and Meta AI made the machine talk to no third-party service, with either question.
  • Four of the eight left proof that they answered: their window title became the answer.

What cannot be claimed

  • That the machine went on to connect to those services, or that it sent anything: what is seen here is that the machine asked for the name. A connection almost always follows, but a browser also looks names up ahead of time and sometimes never uses them.
  • That the four that never change title really searched: they never change it, whether they answer or not.
  • What is sent when there is a connection, or what the answer says: GUARDIANA sees names, not content.
  • That an AI with zero third-party names collects nothing: it can do so through its own domains.
  • That these numbers are anyone’s numbers: they depend on the account, the country and the minute.
  • That Mistral is better or worse: it was not measured signed in.

What you can switch off

  • In any of them: a content blocker stops the measurement tools, but not what the AI asks of its own domains.
  • In Gemini: the advertising it loads belongs to the Google account and is trimmed in Google's ad settings.
  • With GUARDIANA: Guard Mode lets you declare which services an AI needs to talk to and block the rest, with undo.

Data and method

Full data: ocho-ia-buscando-en-internet-2026-09-20.csv (name, category, whether it belongs to the maker and, per AI, queries and whether its page asked for it). Method: each AI opened in its own tab of the everyday Chrome (real profile, sessions already signed in) with site/src/radiografia/ia-teclas.ps1, launched as a scheduled task in the interactive session: name cache emptied before each one, the address typed into the omnibox, 16 seconds of waiting, the question typed, 40 more seconds and the tab closed. From each window are subtracted Windows' names, the signed-in browser's own, the machine's search engines and — written one by one in each AI's note — whatever the browser guessed while the previous address was being typed. The tools are in the program's repository (site/src/radiografia/); anyone can repeat the test with GUARDIANA installed.

Brands named as the names of their owners. If you are the maker and something here is inaccurate, write to hola@guardianagroup.com: it is corrected, with the date.
