Check a receipt

A GUARDIANA ZERO receipt is ZERO's signed record of what it cut in your browser. Here you can check that it has not changed since it was signed.

What a receipt is, in plain words

  1. 1
    You browse with GUARDIANA ZERO

    Every time a page tries to send something to a tracking or advertising company or a data broker, ZERO cuts it before it leaves and writes it down: the time, the page you were on, the company, its country and what the request carried (by the names of its fields: “the page you are on”, “an identifier of yours”, “your screen size”…). It never writes down the values: your data is not written anywhere.

  2. 2
    You ask for the receipt

    In “What was cut”, the “Signed receipt” button saves that list to a file (.json) in your Downloads folder. The file is yours and never leaves your computer.

  3. 3
    ZERO seals it

    When saving it, ZERO puts a digital seal (a signature) on it, made with a key that only exists in your browser. It works like the wax seal on an envelope: if anyone changes a single letter of the file —adds a company, deletes a line or changes a time—, the seal no longer fits.

  4. 4
    You check it here

    This page checks the seal with your own browser's cryptography. Green: the file has not changed since it was signed by the key whose fingerprint the result shows. Red: someone changed it afterwards.

What is it for?

To show someone else what ZERO cut in your browser —a lawyer, your country's data protection authority, a journalist, your employer—, with a signature that shows the file has not changed since it was sealed. Whoever gets it can check it themselves on this page, without installing anything.

What a receipt does not prove

That what it says is true, that the list is complete (the receipt itself says what ZERO cannot see: other apps on the computer, isolated tabs, what each website's servers send on their own), or what each company does with what it gets. The signature says which key sealed it and that nobody changed it afterwards; whoever controls that browser could seal something else.

Check your receipt

The sample is signed with a demonstration key, not anyone's.

The file is not sent anywhere: the check happens in your browser and makes no connection.

The key's fingerprint is like the serial number of the seal. Every browser has its own, shown in GUARDIANA ZERO › Settings. If the receipt's fingerprint matches a ZERO's, the receipt was signed with that browser's key.
