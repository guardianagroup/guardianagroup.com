What it does

  • It blocks what websites call behind your back: trackers, advertising and the companies registered as data brokers. The website you open still works; only the outside companies get blocked.
  • It shows you everything, live. Each tab’s shield says how many companies tried and how many were blocked. “What was blocked” shows every blocked request one by one, with the company, its country, the kind of request and the reason, and saves it as PDF or CSV.
  • What the page tried to report about you. When a shop tells Meta, Google, TikTok and other advertising networks what you did (that you bought something, for how much, sometimes with your hashed email), GUARDIANA ZERO reads it before blocking it and tells you in one sentence. You can save it as an image to share, without amounts or email.
  • Who followed you from site to site. Each day and each month, the companies that showed up on several of your websites, on how many, and whether they were blocked on all of them. It is what a company needs to link your visits into a profile.
  • What ZERO measured on each page. Which scripts tried to read your computer’s fingerprint (canvas, graphics card, audio, fonts) and which company they belonged to; whether the page loads a session recording service and whether it sent it data after you typed; whether your hashed email travelled in an address to another company, and how many uploads went out to other companies. It all stays in memory and is cleared when you close.
  • Maximum protection in one button: on top of the above, it blocks other companies’ telemetry and the pings a page sends to another company only to carry information out; it blocks session recording services and gives other companies’ scripts a different fingerprint when they try to read yours. If a site asks for more checks because of it, unblock that company. Every site can be unblocked and blocked again from the shield.
  • It removes tracking tags from addresses (utm, gclid, fbclid…) before they leave.
  • It rejects cookie notices from the most used consent managers (OneTrust, Cookiebot, Didomi and others) by clicking “Reject all”; if the notice has no such button, it hides it without accepting anything. It can be turned off in Settings.
  • It removes pop-ups. It does not open the windows a page opens on its own or when you click anywhere on it, nor those that lead to advertising or tracking; the link you click does open. It closes the boxes a page puts on top asking for your email, to turn on notifications or to get its app, and says “no” to pages that ask for notifications before you press anything. If something you wanted did not open, the shield lets you open it.
  • A profile of every company. Tap a company’s name to see where it is from, the addresses it uses and what it tried with you in the last month. For the 49 we have already checked (Google, Meta, Criteo, ByteDance, The Trade Desk…), you also see what it does, what it earned, its fines and sanctions and its data breaches, and for the biggest ones where it is incorporated, its taxes, its antitrust cases and how it turns your data into money, each fact with its public source. From the profile you can download, as a PDF, the letter asking it to delete your data under your country’s law, or open it in your email. You can share it and save it as a PDF.
  • Your favourites, with the star in the address bar, as quick links on the new tab. You can bring yours from Chrome, Edge or Brave in one click. They are kept only on your computer.
  • Your marked data (your email, your phone…): GUARDIANA ZERO stops it going out to another company when it detects it, even turned into code the way ad networks do, and before a form sends it, it asks you.
  • A task for your AI: a tab that only goes to the websites you name, for as long as you allow, and leaves a signed receipt of what it did.
  • Redaction: prepares what you are going to ask an AI with your personal data struck out, and puts it back in place in the answer.

What it does not do

  • It does not make you anonymous, least of all to a website where you are signed in, and it does not stop every way of recognising a computer by its fingerprint.
  • It is not a VPN: the websites you visit still see your IP address.
  • It only sees what goes through the browser. What the other programs on the computer do is seen by GUARDIANA, the program.
  • The web engine is WebView2, by Microsoft, the same as Edge’s: it comes with Windows 10 and 11 and Windows keeps it up to date.

What it costs

The first 7 days are free, with no card and no account, and they count separately from GUARDIANA’s. After that, GUARDIANA ZERO comes with the GUARDIANA subscription: the same key works for both programs. If the trial ends without a subscription, the browser keeps opening pages, but without protection: it does not block trackers, advertising or data brokers, clean addresses, watch your marked data, or do tasks for your AI or redactions; only the web engine’s basic protection remains, the same as Microsoft Edge’s. GUARDIANA ZERO does not use SmartScreen, Microsoft’s filter against fraudulent sites and dangerous downloads, during the trial or after it, because it would send the addresses you visit to Microsoft. To activate the key it connects once to Dodo Payments, the payment gateway, and after that checks it 8 days later and then once per billing period (every 30 days with the Founder licence); if a subscription goes 7 days without being checked, protection switches off until a check succeeds; and if you press “Check for updates”, it reads the public ledger on GitHub once. Every connection is shown, with its date, under “Subscription”. If anything looks wrong, write to hola@guardianagroup.com: it gets corrected, with the date.
