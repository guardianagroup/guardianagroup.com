THE ZERO BROWSER · DEMONSTRATION VERSION

Each website opens far from your device, and you decide what gets cut.

ZERO opens each website in an isolated browser, the “bubble”, which runs on a GUARDIANA server in Nuremberg (Germany) or in Miami (United States): whichever responds first. Only images of the page reach your device, and the website sees the server’s IP address, not yours. In the GUARDIANA program for Windows, ZERO opens from Orbit, just as on the website: it is the same service. You can also open it now in your browser.

What it does

  • It opens the website in the bubble, not on your device. The page runs on the GUARDIANA server and only images of it reach your device. Websites, and the DNS service that the server uses to find them, see the server’s IP address (or that of the Tor network, if you choose it), not yours.
  • You choose how to search. You type a website’s address or search for it with Google, with Brave or, if you choose, through the Tor network.
  • It goes in before you. It checks malware and fraud lists, the certificate, the downloads and the letters that imitate others. If there is a serious risk, it does not go in; if there is a sign of risk, it warns you and you choose.
  • It shows you who is behind each connection. You see which company it is, which country it is from and what it is for, and you can cut it with a switch. With Google and with Brave it cuts nothing by default, except dangerous websites: you choose. With Tor, it cuts advertising and tracking from the start.
  • It plants decoys. Each bubble carries marked decoy data. If any of it goes out to another company, ZERO shows it to you with the evidence: which piece of data went out, where to and when.
  • It destroys the bubble. When you close the tab, after 30 minutes with the tab hidden, after 12 hours or when you press “Destroy”, the bubble disappears along with its cookies, its cache and its tabs. Before that, a record summarises what ZERO did during the session. Downloads wait in the bubble until you request them or discard them.
  • It sums it up for you. What ZERO detected and what it cut (the website, the company, its country and the time; up to 5,000 entries) is kept only in your browser. NÚCLEO shows it to you and prepares PDF or video summaries from it; “Delete” in NÚCLEO removes it.

What it does not do

  • It does not hide what you do from the GUARDIANA server. To run the bubble for you, the server receives the addresses and searches you type, your clicks, movements and keystrokes (also what you type into a form, passwords included) and the text you paste, as well as your IP address, like any connection. Everything lives in your session’s memory, and the service is configured not to record the websites you visit or your IP address in any log; that configuration is in the public code.
  • It does not hide your searches from Google. If you search with Google, the search is carried out by Google from your own browser: it receives what you search for, your IP address and whatever its cookies collect. The results open in the bubble; Google’s ads do not: your browser opens them. With Brave or with Tor, the search is carried out by the bubble.
  • It is not a VPN. Only what you open in ZERO goes through the bubble; the other programs on your computer go out to the internet as usual. In the program, “Your PC” shows those connections.
  • It does not catch everything. It recognises what is on its lists and in GUARDIANA’s database; when it does not know which company is behind a connection, it says so. No filter detects all the tracking, fraud or dangerous content on the internet.
  • It is not a final version. ZERO is a demonstration version: it may be slow or fail on some websites.

What each server receives and how long it is kept is set out in the privacy policy (point 4), and how it works on the inside, in How it works.
