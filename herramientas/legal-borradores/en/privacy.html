PRIVACY POLICY · 28 OCTOBER 2026

No account, no cookies and no data sold. This is what each service sees.

GUARDIANA works from this website: you do not have to give us your name or your email address to use it, it uses no cookies or analytics, and we do not sell or share data. Even so, some services need a server: the ZERO browser and Communications go through GUARDIANA servers, and some Orbit measurements query other companies, only when you ask for them. This page explains, service by service, what leaves your browser, who sees it and how long it is kept.

1. This website

The website is served from GitHub Pages, by GitHub, Inc. Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address, the browser you use and the page you request; what it keeps is explained in its privacy statement. The pages load nothing from other companies: fonts, images and libraries are served from guardianagroup.com. The only exceptions are those in points 3 and 4, and only when you use that feature.

The website uses no cookies. In your browser’s storage it keeps the language, your Orbit and ZERO preferences and, if you use them, what points 3, 4 and 5 explain. None of that is sent to GUARDIANA. To delete all of it, delete the site data for guardianagroup.com in your browser’s settings.

2. NÚCLEO

NÚCLEO runs entirely in your browser: it understands what you ask without sending it to any server or to any artificial intelligence. To take you from one section to another, it remembers the steps of the guide, only in that tab and for one hour at most; they may include part of what you typed, except anything that looks like an email address or a long number. The PDF and video summaries are made in your browser and are not sent to anyone.

3. Orbit and the X-ray

  • The map. The planet’s data is downloaded from this website, and the satellites’ positions are calculated in your browser.
  • What any website knows about you (browser, system, language, time and screen) is read in your browser and is not sent.
  • “Measure from outside”. Only if you press it, your browser queries IPinfo (ipinfo.io), which receives your IP address and returns your internet provider and your approximate location.
  • “Measure routes” and “The journey of your data”. Only if you press them, your browser makes about 140 brief requests to Cloudflare and to 33 Amazon Web Services regions to measure how long the round trip takes. Those companies receive your IP address, your browser and the time, as on any visit; what they keep is explained in their privacy policies.
  • “Set location”. If you ask for it, your browser works out where you are, with your system’s location service. Orbit keeps the city and the coordinates, rounded to about 100 metres, only in your browser, and “Forget” deletes them.

The results stay on the page, and in the report if you download it.

4. The ZERO browser

ZERO opens each website in an isolated browser, the “bubble”, which runs on a GUARDIANA server in Nuremberg (Germany), hosted by Hetzner Online GmbH. Only images of the page reach your device. Websites, and the DNS service the server uses to find them, see the server’s IP address (or the Tor network’s, if you choose it), not yours.

  • What the server receives. The addresses and searches you type, your clicks, pointer movements and keystrokes (including what you type into a form, passwords included) and the text you paste. It needs them to do in the bubble what you would do in your browser. Like any connection, it also receives your IP address.
  • For how long. Everything lives in your session’s memory and is deleted when you close the tab, after 30 minutes with the tab hidden, after 12 hours or when you press “Destroy”. The service is configured not to write the websites you visit or your IP address to any log, and that configuration is in the public code. Downloads wait in the bubble until you ask for them or discard them.
  • Searches. With Google, the search is done by Google from your own browser, with its Programmable Search service: Google receives what you search for, your IP address and whatever its cookies collect, under its privacy policy. The results open in the bubble; Google’s ads do not: your browser opens them. With Brave or with Tor, the search is done by the bubble, and Brave sees the IP address of the server or of the Tor network.
  • Decoys. Each bubble carries decoy data. If someone visits a decoy link, the server records the IP address and the browser of whoever visited it, to show it to you in your session.
  • In your browser. ZERO keeps what it detected and what it cut (the website, the company, its country and the time; up to 5,000 entries) to show it to you in NÚCLEO and in the summaries. “Delete” in NÚCLEO removes it.

5. Communications

Messages, voice notes and files are encrypted in your browser, end to end: the GUARDIANA server that carries them (the relay, in Nuremberg) cannot read them.

  • What the relay sees. When you open the page, your browser connects to the relay, even if you are not talking to anyone. The relay sees your IP address, the room (an identifier without a name), the times and the volume of data. It keeps no messages, names or keys, and its code writes no logs: what it needs, it holds in memory while the conversation lasts.
  • The invitation. With a 9-digit code, the conversation’s secret waits on the relay, locked with a key derived from the code, for 10 minutes or until it is used. Its protection depends on that code: that is why it expires quickly and works only once. In the shielded session, the secret travels in the link or in the QR code and does not go through the relay.
  • Calls and video calls. They always go through GUARDIANA’s call server, which sees both people’s IP addresses, the times and the volume, not the audio or the image, and is configured without logs. The camera and the microphone are used only during the call and with your permission. If you change your voice or protect your image (“Blurred” or “Mosaic”), the change is made on your device before encryption: the original does not leave it.
  • What the other person receives. What you send them, the name you enter and the country your browser infers from your time zone, which appears in the files and in the receipt.
  • In your browser. Your identity (keys that never leave it) and your name; the messages, stored encrypted, and the files received, unencrypted, until you press “End”, which deletes them here and requests their deletion on the other device. Your identity and your name stay until you delete the site data.
  • The delivery receipt is signed by both browsers; it carries the names, the countries, the times and the files’ hashes, and it is downloaded to your device: it is not stored on any server.

6. X-rays, Fines and the other pages

They are pages for reading: they request nothing from anyone beyond what point 1 explains.

7. If you write to us

If you write to hola@guardianagroup.com, we keep that email to answer you and for nothing else. It is deleted a year after the last reply, or sooner if you ask.

8. What we do not do

We do not sell or share data. There is no advertising, no analytics, no accounts and no profiles of the people who use the website. Nothing is charged on the website today, so we do not process payment data.

9. Your rights, legal basis and complaints

You can ask us to tell you what data of yours we hold, to correct it or to delete it, by writing to hola@guardianagroup.com. Almost everything that points 2 to 5 explain lives only in your browser or in the memory of a session that has already ended, so it does not exist anywhere we could look for it. We process your email because you wrote to us (your consent, which you can withdraw whenever you want), and the connection data because it is needed to provide the service you ask for. The ZERO and Communications server is in Germany; GitHub, which serves the website, is in the United States. If you think we are mishandling your data, you can complain to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (Colombia), the ANPD (Brazil), the AEPD (Spain) or the data protection authority of your country; before that, write to us and we will sort it out. Data controller: GUARDIANA GROUP S.A.S. (NIT 902.119.954-6), Torre Empresarial PRODEGI, Carrera 3 # 9-30, 18th floor, Bocagrande, Cartagena de Indias, Colombia.

10. Annex: the desktop program and the launch list

The GUARDIANA program for Windows and macOS and the GUARDIANA ZERO browser for Windows are no longer sold or available for download. If you have them installed, if you bought a licence or if you joined the launch list, what points A1 to A7 say still applies; they are the points of the previous version of this policy.

A1. The launch list

The list closed on 28 September 2026, the day version 1.0 came out: nobody new can join. If you signed up, we do not store that address: it is stored by beehiiv, Inc., a company in the United States, which is also who sends the emails. Their servers are in the United States. Together with what the payment gateway keeps if you buy (point A5), it is the only personal data of GUARDIANA that lives outside your machine.

  • What is stored. Your email address, the date you signed up, and whether you open the emails we send or click a link inside them. That last part is measured by the provider on its own and cannot be switched off; we say so because it is true. What could be switched off, we switched off on 22 September 2026: the automatic tagging that added tracking marks to every link. And those open and click figures are not used for anything: not to decide who to write to, not to segment, and they are not crossed with anything. We ask for no name, no country and no phone number.
  • What for. To tell you on launch day and to report what changes in the program. Never third-party advertising. The list is not sold, not shared, and never crossed with anything from the program: there would be no way, because the program sends nothing.
  • You have to confirm. After signing up, an email arrives asking you to confirm. If you do not confirm, we do not write to you.
  • How to leave. Every email has an unsubscribe link at the bottom, and it works immediately. You can also write to hola@guardianagroup.com and I will do it.
  • Deletion. If you ask for your address to be deleted, it is deleted from the whole list, not just from the mailings.

Whoever signed up did so there, on the provider’s page, under its own privacy policy. This website no longer has that button.

A2. The program

GUARDIANA writes down, on your machine, the names of the services each device in your home tries to contact, the time, the device and the category. On Windows, for this computer’s own queries, it also writes down which program asked for each name: its name, its path on disk (which may include your user name) and the file’s fingerprint. Since 1.0.22, on Windows, it also records how much data each program sends to and receives from each destination, minute by minute: bytes, packets, protocol and port (the antivirus’s only while “GUARDIANA watches my antivirus” is on). Never the content of what you do: GUARDIANA does not read or decrypt what travels. Answers are never stored; only the country and network of the server that answered, to tell you where your data travels: GUARDIANA looks them up at that moment in a public database that comes inside the program (DB-IP Lite), without asking anyone, and does not store the server’s address. And when a name presents itself as part of a website but answers as another company’s tracker, the name of that tracker’s site is written down too (for example, omtrdc.net), never an address. If you use “Make them delete you” or “Case files”, it also keeps which company you wrote to, under which law, what you asked for (to delete your data or to tell you what it holds), the date of each step, what it answered and the notes you write yourself; your name and email, never (only in your browser, if you tick “Remember them in this browser”). That ledger lives on your PC (Windows: C:\ProgramData\Guardiana; macOS: /Library/Application Support/Guardiana), it is yours, and you can export it and delete it completely from the panel (“What GUARDIANA knows about you”). Whoever administers that PC can read it: GUARDIANA does not encrypt it or hide it from the person who administers the machine, and its threat model says so. When you uninstall, the ledger stays on your disk until you delete it, and so does the trial mark (the date the trial started and the last clock time the program saw) and, if you activated a licence, a copy of the key; never your name or your email. That mark is in HKLM\SOFTWARE\Guardiana (Windows) or in /etc/guardiana/ (Mac), and an administrator can delete it.

The program contacts no server of ours. The only outgoing connections are the licence ones, with the payment gateway: activating it when you type your key and, after that, checking it. A subscription is checked 8 days after activation and then once per billing period; a Lifetime licence, 8 days after activation and then every 30 days. If a check cannot be made, the program tries again every hour. Each one is written into the panel with its date, destination and size.

A3. GUARDIANA ZERO for Windows

GUARDIANA ZERO, the browser for Windows, keeps on your machine only: the log of what it cut (“What was cut”, kept for 31 days: for each request, the name it went to, the path without its values, how many parameters it carried and, since ZERO 1.0.13, the names of those parameters, never their values, to tell you what it carried: the page you were on, an identifier, your screen size), each day’s figures and, also for 31 days, which websites you opened and which tracking, advertising or data broker companies showed up on each (for “Who followed you from site to site”; never from an isolated tab), the “Who has my data” book, the data you mark for it to watch, your favourites, your settings, the licence and trial marks (the date the trial started, which is also stored in a second place), the signed receipts of each mandate and the key this computer signs them with, a file of technical errors (registro.txt) and the web engine’s profile, with the cookies, the cache and what the websites you visit store (isolated tabs and mandate tabs keep none of that once closed). None of it reaches us, because there is no server of ours, and none of it is sent to anyone else; only each website’s cookies and stored data go back to that same website when you visit it, as in any browser. “Delete everything” deletes all of the above except your favourites, your settings and the licence and trial marks; until you press it, the web engine’s profile is kept.

ZERO itself only connects to the pages you open and, for the licence, to the payment gateway, Dodo Payments: to activate your key and to check it under the same rules as GUARDIANA (a subscription, 8 days after activation and then once per billing period; a Lifetime licence, 8 days after activation and then every 30 days; if the gateway does not answer, it tries again every hour); and, only if you press “Check for updates”, it reads the public version ledger on GitHub once. If you bring favourites from Chrome, Edge or Brave, it reads their favourites file on your computer once, and nothing else. Each of those connections is written down, with its date, in the program’s “Subscription” view. There is no telemetry, no server of ours and no account. The web engine, Microsoft’s WebView2, is kept up to date by Windows, not by ZERO.

A4. Home Mode

With Home Mode, your PC answers the name queries of the other devices on your Wi‑Fi. The household panel shows totals per device; the detail of a phone is visible only from that phone, unless its owner shares it. GUARDIANA is not parental control and does not watch people.

A5. Buying

Payment happens on the page of Dodo Payments (checkout.dodopayments.com), the gateway that acts as merchant of record: it is the one that receives your payment and billing details, issues the invoice and handles taxes, under its own privacy policy. We name it because you have a right to know who invoices you. Dodo keeps your name, your email and the details of your purchase, and also your licence key and its activations; on every activation and every check it sees the IP address your computer connects from. We do not see your card; in Dodo’s dashboard we see your name, your email, what you bought and your key. When you activate a licence with a key, the program (GUARDIANA or ZERO) connects to the gateway to check it, and after that checks it again as point A2 says; every connection is written into your ledger.

A6. Your rights over this data

We hold nothing from your machine: it is all there and you control it, so there is nothing to ask us for and nothing to delete. Outside your machine there can be two things: your email address, if you joined the list or wrote to us, and, if you bought, what Dodo Payments keeps about your purchase and your licence (point A5), which we see in its dashboard. Ask to see what we hold or see about you, or to have your email deleted, and it is done the same day; what Dodo keeps is handled by Dodo, under its own privacy policy. For any question: hola@guardianagroup.com. Data controller: GUARDIANA GROUP S.A.S. (NIT 902.119.954-6), Torre Empresarial PRODEGI, Carrera 3 # 9-30, 18th floor, Bocagrande, Cartagena de Indias, Colombia.

A7. Legal basis, retention and complaints

We process your email address because you wrote to us or joined the list (your consent, which you can withdraw whenever you want), and the details of your purchase because they are needed to deliver what you bought and for the invoice (a tax obligation). The address on the list is kept until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it; the email of a conversation is deleted a year after the last reply, or sooner if you ask. What Dodo keeps about a purchase, Dodo keeps under its privacy policy and for as long as tax law requires. beehiiv is in the United States; beehiiv and Dodo Payments say in their privacy policies where they keep your data and under which safeguards they transfer it (for example, standard contractual clauses). If you think we are mishandling your data, you can complain to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (Colombia), the ANPD (Brazil), the AEPD (Spain) or the data protection authority of your country; before that, write to us and we will fix it.
